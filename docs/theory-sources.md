# Theory sources and assignment coverage

The Musicianship III–IV sequence is an original practice path, not an official
course syllabus. Instructional explanations and examples were checked against:

- [Open Music Theory: Strengthening Endings with V7](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheorycopy/chapter/strengthening-endings-with-v7/): leading-tone and chordal-seventh behavior, complete/incomplete voicings, inner-voice leading-tone drop.
- [Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom: Voice Leading Seventh Chords](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/VoiceLeadingSeventhChordsIntro.html): the ordinary downward-by-step resolution of a chordal seventh.
- [Open Music Theory: Tonicization](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/tonicization/): applied chords and temporary leading tones, including context-dependent exceptions.
- [Open Music Theory: Extended Tonicization and Modulation](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/extended-tonicization-and-modulation-to-closely-related-keys/): tonal evidence and pivot-chord interpretation.
- [Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/MusicTheory.html): chapters on non-chord tones, phrases, mixture, Neapolitan and augmented-sixth chords provide further study.

The bundled music21 library handles expanded chord-symbol interpretation offline.
The app's SATB engine then applies its own explicit classroom profile. Accepting
a chord symbol does not certify that every style treats it with chorale rules.
The four-voice reduction policy for extended chords is a documented application
choice, not a universal orchestration rule. Enharmonic spelling is preserved in
constraints and checked independently before a solution is displayed.

## Relationship to Auralia and Musition

The September 2026 expansion consulted the user's
[Solve Auralia and Musition combined skill](https://github.com/Eipckz/codex-skills/blob/main/skills/solve-auralia-musition-combined/SKILL.md)
and its historical audit matrix as requirements evidence. The app does not
connect to those services or submit assignments. Historical feedback and
partially solved examples were not treated as a universal answer key.

| Assignment need | Available workflow |
| --- | --- |
| Partial SATB, supplied soprano or bass, mixed voice clues | Part Writing table; paste aligned rows; lock notes; solve/check |
| Roman numerals, inversion, figured bass, applied chords | Explicit labels, figure row, exact bass and per-slot alternatives |
| Longer progressions | Add/duplicate/reorder without a fixed slot cap; bounded/exhaustive search controls |
| A chord outside the original menu | Chord-symbol parser or root-first `notes:` collection; explicit required/forbidden tones |
| Modes, tendency-tone resolution, chromatic function | Seven Musicianship III–IV skills in Learn and Practice |
| Ear training, transcription, keyboard practice | Existing Aural/Dictation/Piano exercises |
| Rhythmic suspensions, ties, rests, species counterpoint realization | Studied in lessons; not automatically generated by this chord-snapshot solver |
| More than four simultaneous independent parts | Not supported; extended chord symbols are reduced to four voices |
| Modulation across a score | Lessons cover interpretation; solve separate local-key sections |

A valid completion may differ from an instructor's intended answer. Select
the instructor's constraints and rule severities, compare alternatives, and
read diagnostics. No result is claimed for an interrupted or exhausted search.

## Assignment calculators (v1.2)

- [Open Music Theory: rhythmic values](https://open-musictheory.github.io/docs/fundamentals/rhythmic-values/) supports dot arithmetic; each successive dot adds half the preceding addition. The calculator measures durations only and does not validate ties between pitches.
- [Open Music Theory: compound meters](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/compound-meters-and-time-signatures/) explains three-part beat divisions and compound duple/triple/quadruple grouping. The calculator distinguishes bar length from perceived pulse, and leaves irregular grouping to musical context.
- Note analysis preserves spelling and reports a possible key-dependent music21 interpretation, not a unique harmonic function. A fragment cannot establish missing chord members or voice-leading.
